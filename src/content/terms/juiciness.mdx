The word comes from game development, and its clearest statement is the talk Martin Jonasson
and Petri Purho gave called Juice it or Lose it, where they took a plain Breakout clone and
piled response onto it until it was a pleasure to touch. Their phrase for the principle is
maximum output for minimum input: one small act from the player, an enormous amount of
answer from the game. Juicy things, as the write-up of that talk puts it, are things that
wobble, squirt, bounce around and make little noises. Nothing in the stack is individually
clever. What makes it juiciness is that there are six of them at once, on an act that would
have been perfectly usable with one.

That is the definition worth holding onto, because it is what separates the word from every
individual effect it contains. [Squash and stretch](/squash-and-stretch) is one deformation.
[Overshoot](/overshoot) is one curve that goes past its mark. A
[confetti burst](/confetti-burst) is one celebration. Juiciness is the decision to run all of
them off the same press, along with a count that bumps, a colour that flashes, a ring that
expands, an [earcon](/earcon), and a tick of [haptic feedback](/haptic-feedback). The layering
is the technique: each response is cheap and legible on its own, and the pile of them
converts a click into something that feels like it happened in the world rather than in a
state machine.

Now the honest part, because this is the word for what a team asking to "add some
microinteractions" actually wants. Juiciness is a matter of degree, and the correct amount
is almost never the maximum. It belongs on acts that are rare and worth marking: finishing
setup, sending the thing, hitting the streak, winning. It is wrong on any act somebody
performs forty times a day, where every millisecond of performance is a millisecond of
waiting, and the wobble that delighted on Monday is an obstacle by Thursday. It is wrong
where precision matters, since a control that squashes under the finger is a control that
moved. And it is wrong for anyone who has asked it to stop:
[prefers-reduced-motion](/prefers-reduced-motion) is not a hint here, it is the whole stack
turned off, with the state change kept and the celebration dropped. Sound and haptics need
the same escape hatch.

The vocabulary of games is already all over interface design, and this is one of its more
useful imports, alongside [attract mode](/attract-mode), the
[diegetic interface](/diegetic-interface) and the deliberate wiggle of
[jiggle mode](/jiggle-mode). Used as a diagnosis it is sharp: ask how many responses one
press produces, and whether each of them is doing work. One is often too few, because a
control with no answer at all reads as broken. Six is a party, and a party is only
appropriate when there is something to celebrate.
