A scarcity indicator is a count, published where a decision is being made. "3 left in
this size." "2 seats at this fare." "Booking closes in 40 minutes." It is one of the few
pieces of merchandising copy that is genuinely useful to the reader: someone weighing a
purchase is trying to work out whether they can come back tomorrow, and the count
answers exactly that question. It is placed beside the action rather than in the product
details because it is only relevant at the moment of committing, and it belongs to the
specific thing being bought, not to the catalogue.

One sentence divides this from its counterfeit. An honest indicator reports a number the
seller actually has. [Fake scarcity](/fake-scarcity) manufactures pressure from nothing,
and [fake urgency](/fake-urgency) does the same to the clock. Four practical tests
separate them, and all four are about whether the number is attached to something real.
It moves when the world moves and holds still when the world does not, so a refresh
never reseeds it. It names its unit and its scope, which is why "3 left in size M" is a
claim and "high demand" is not. It can reach zero and say so, since a count that never
runs out was never counting. And the interface would still print it if the news were
bad, which is the honesty question underneath the other three.

Because the truthful version and the manipulative version look identical in a
screenshot, most of the design work is in the plumbing rather than the styling. Read the
figure from inventory at request time, scope it to the variant the reader is looking at,
and let sold out be a first-class state rather than a disappearance. Keep the warning
colour for the case that is genuinely running out, and remember it cannot carry the
meaning alone: the words have to say it too. Reserve the line's space in the layout so
the count arriving does not shove the buy button under the reader's thumb, and announce
it politely rather than interrupting, since it is a status and not an error. Where the
constraint is time rather than quantity, the same rules apply to a
[countdown timer](/countdown-timer): a real deadline, or none.

The commercial argument for restraint is that the honest indicator is the one that keeps
working. A count that is right teaches the reader that this shop's numbers mean
something, which is worth more on the second visit than a percentage point of conversion
on the first. Once every listing on a site says "only 2 left", the sentence has been
spent, and nothing is left to say on the day there really are only two.
