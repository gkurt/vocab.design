Hyperreal 3D is what happens when a product page hires a render instead of a photographer.
One object, modelled and lit in a 3D package, comes out with physically based materials, a
chrome band picking up an environment map, subsurface scattering warming the shadow side,
crisp [specular highlights](/specular-highlight), and a contact shadow that grounds it on
nothing at all. It is then floated over a plain type layout, because the object is doing all
the visual work and anything else on the page would compete with it. The word hyperreal is
doing real duty: the render is not trying to pass as a photograph, it is trying to be
cleaner, glossier and more perfectly lit than a photograph could be.

The contrast that matters most is with [neo-skeuomorphism](/neo-skeuomorphism), because both
look expensive and glossy and they are doing opposite jobs. Neo-skeuomorphism describes the
material a *control* appears to be made of, so its gloss is an affordance argument: this
thing is a button, it is soft, it will give when you press it. Hyperreal 3D is an *image*,
an illustration hero that is not a control at all, answers to no affordance, and would be
lying if it invited a press. Put plainly: if the glossy thing is meant to be tapped, you are
looking at neo-skeuomorphism, and if it is meant to be looked at, you are looking at
hyperreal 3D.

The register has a house vocabulary you can read off almost any example. A
[blob shape](/blob-shape) or a soft-body form rather than a hard product silhouette, since
an organic surface shows off the lighting model. [Gloss](/gloss) laid on as a real material
response rather than as a highlight painted on top, so the bright band moves with the
implied light source instead of sitting where a designer thought it looked good. And a
palette drawn from the object rather than the brand, which is why these pages so often go
violet and peach: those are the hues that flatter a subsurface glow.

The costs are unglamorous and worth stating before you commit. A hero render is a large
asset, and the versions that look best are the ones that ship at several sizes and in two
themes, which multiplies the production rather than the file. It cannot be typed, translated
or restyled by a token, so it dates the way photography dates. It needs alt text that
describes the product rather than the render. And a rendered object placed near real
controls will out-shout every one of them, which is a composition problem rather than a
taste problem: the object earns its place at the top of a page precisely because there is
nothing near it to lose the contest.
