Every part of this look is a specific failure of magnetic tape. A tracking band is the
horizontal strip of torn, noisy picture that rolls through the frame when the head is not
aligned with the recorded track. Colour smears past the edges of objects because tape
carried far less bandwidth for chroma than for brightness, so the hues arrive late and
blurred while the outlines stay sharp. Comb teeth appear along fast horizontal motion
because the two interlaced fields of a frame were recorded a fiftieth of a second apart.
White flecks are dropouts, places where the oxide has shed. And everything is a little soft,
a little washed, and a little warped, because each copy of a copy loses more. On top of the
picture, not in it, sits the recorder's own display: PLAY with a triangle, REC with a dot, a
running counter, and a date stamp in a coarse bitmap face.

The look is a costume made of those faults, and it belongs to the home camcorder rather
than to broadcast. Consumer VHS arrived in the late 1970s, took the format war from Betamax
on tape length and price, and put a recording device in ordinary houses for about fifteen
years. What is being imitated now is not television, it is somebody's own tape: a birthday,
a garden, a school corridor, filmed by hand and rewatched until it degraded. That is why
the revival landed in horror. Analog horror works by presenting itself as recovered
footage, and the artifacts do the arguing, because a viewer reads decay as age and age as
evidence.

Two neighbours are commonly confused with it. The [glitch aesthetic](/glitch-aesthetic) is
digital: block artifacts, datamosh, bit errors, an image failing as data. This one is
analog: nothing is corrupted, the signal has simply worn out. [Scanlines](/scanlines) are
the raster of the tube the tape was played back on, an overlay that can be dropped over any
picture, which is why the comb tearing in the specimen here is kept inside the tracking band
where the format actually puts it. [Vaporwave](/vaporwave) borrows all of this as period
detail, and [cassette futurism](/cassette-futurism) is the hardware the tape went into
rather than the image that came out.

For interface work it is decoration and should be built as such: an
[aria-hidden](/aria-hidden) layer with no pointer events over an image, never a filter over
running text. Chroma fringing destroys legibility, so keep the offset shadows on display
lettering and leave body copy clean. The rolling band is the risky part, since a bright
strip travelling through a frame on a loop is both distracting and exactly the pattern that
photosensitivity guidance addresses, so keep it slow, keep it low contrast, and give
[reduced motion](/prefers-reduced-motion) a version that simply rests. The overlay text is a
prop, so any real timestamp still needs to exist in the markup as text.
