The sender name is the first field a reader uses and the last one a sender thinks about.
It is set once, in a configuration screen, usually by whoever set up the sending tool,
and then it introduces every mail the company ever sends. Scanning a list, a person
resolves the name before the [subject line](/subject-line) has been read at all: the
decision is recognition, not interest, and a name nobody recognises turns a good subject
into an unopened row.

Three choices cover almost every case. A person ("Ana Ruiz") reads as correspondence and
invites a reply, at the cost of being unrecognisable to anyone who does not already know
Ana. A brand ("Quay Books") is recognised instantly, which is also the risk: it files
cleanly as marketing. A person at a brand ("Ana at Quay Books") buys recognition and a
human at once, and it is the longest, which matters in a field that gets less room than
any other line in the row.

The name is also artwork, because clients derive a picture from it. With no avatar
published, the initials of the from name become the [avatar](/avatar) in the row, so
"Quay Books" draws QB and "Ana at Quay Books" draws A. Changing the name therefore
changes the shape of the thing a reader has learned to spot in a list, which is the real
cost of a rename: recognition is the asset the field exists to hold, and it resets. Teams
that must rename usually ship the old name and the new one together for a while, in the
name field rather than only in the body.

Two fences are worth stating. This is the display name in the row, not the address
behind it, and a client that shows both is showing two different fields. And whether the
name can be trusted at all is a separate matter settled by mail authentication rather
than by design: a from name is a label, it is not evidence, and no amount of care in
choosing it makes an unauthenticated mail trustworthy.
