A disguised ad is an advertisement wearing the interface's own clothes. On a download page
it is the biggest, greenest, most confident Download button on the screen, and the real
link is a grey line of text underneath. In a feed it is the third card, set in the same
typeface at the same size with the same byline shape as the two around it. The mechanism is
identical in both: the click was aimed at something the reader believed the product was
offering, and it was collected by someone who paid for the resemblance.

The specimen above runs one feed card three ways. Undisguised it is indistinguishable from
its neighbours. Then it carries the disclosure most of them actually carry, the word
sponsored set in eight grey pixels at the end of a meta line, which is worth naming for
what it is: a disclosure that exists technically and not practically. It can be pointed at
in a compliance review and it cannot be read at arm's length on a phone, and that gap is
not an accident of typography, it is the entire product. The third state shows what an
honest label costs, which is contrast, position, and a name. The label comes first rather
than last, the card is tinted so it stops belonging to the column it sits in, and the
advertiser is named where the publisher would be.

None of this is an argument against advertising in a feed. Paid placement can be perfectly
legible, and readers are considerably more tolerant of it than publishers assume. The
question is only whether the reader can tell before they click, which is also the legal
standard in most places, since advertising rules generally require disclosure that is clear
and conspicuous rather than merely present. [Deceptive
Design](https://www.deceptive.design/types) files this under its own name for the same
reason: the pattern is defined by the reader's mistake, so a label the reader cannot see is
the same product as no label at all.

Two neighbours name the wider behaviour. [Dark pattern](/dark-pattern) is the family, the
whole class of interfaces built to produce a choice the reader would not have made with the
facts in front of them, and a disguised ad is one of its oldest members.
[Misdirection](/misdirection) is the technique inside it, attention aimed at the thing that
looks like the answer, and the disguise is only the most literal way to do that. On the
layout side, [feed layout](/feed-layout) is the uniform column of like-shaped items that
makes the disguise cheap, since a card only has to match its neighbours to disappear, and
[metered paywall](/metered-paywall) is the other thing a publisher reaches for when the
same page has to earn money, with the difference that a paywall announces itself.
